# Dietary Reference Intakes: Energy and Protein for Infants and Young Children

Applies to: children from birth to 60 months. Source: National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (formerly Institute of Medicine), *Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, Carbohydrate, Fiber, Fat, Fatty Acids, Cholesterol, Protein, and Amino Acids*, 2005, as summarised in the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements nutrient recommendation tables.

## What the DRIs are

Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs) are the reference values used in the United States and Canada. The **Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA)** meets the needs of nearly all healthy people in an age group; where evidence is insufficient, an **Adequate Intake (AI)** is given instead. For energy, the DRIs provide an **Estimated Energy Requirement (EER)** — an equation based on age, sex, weight, length/height and activity — rather than a single number, because energy needs depend so strongly on body size. These are population reference values; an individual child's needs are judged by growth.

## Energy (calories)

Infant energy needs per kilogram are the highest of any life stage and fall steadily through early childhood.

Estimated Energy Requirement equations (2005 DRI), where weight is in kg and length/height in metres:

- **0–3 months:** EER = (89 × weight − 100) + 175 kcal/day
- **4–6 months:** EER = (89 × weight − 100) + 56
- **7–12 months:** EER = (89 × weight − 100) + 22
- **13–35 months:** EER = (89 × weight − 100) + 20
- **3–8 years, boys:** EER = 88.5 − 61.9 × age(years) + PA × (26.7 × weight + 903 × height) + 20
- **3–8 years, girls:** EER = 135.3 − 30.8 × age(years) + PA × (10.0 × weight + 934 × height) + 20

(PA is a physical-activity coefficient: 1.00 sedentary, 1.13/1.16 low-active, 1.26/1.31 active, 1.42/1.56 very active for boys/girls.) The added constants (175, 56, 22, 20) are the energy deposited in new tissue during growth.

Typical resulting ranges, for orientation only:

- **0–6 months:** roughly 500–700 kcal/day (about 100–110 kcal per kg)
- **7–12 months:** roughly 600–900 kcal/day (about 80–95 kcal per kg)
- **1–2 years:** roughly 700–1,000 kcal/day
- **2–3 years:** roughly 1,000–1,400 kcal/day
- **4 years:** roughly 1,200–1,600 kcal/day

Because these depend on an individual child's weight, height, sex and activity, calculated values should be produced from the equations with the child's actual measurements rather than read from a general table.

## Protein

Protein requirements are set per kilogram of body weight and, for convenience, as a daily amount for a reference-size child:

| Age | Reference value | Per kg body weight | Per day (reference child) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–6 months | AI | 1.52 g/kg | 9.1 g |
| 7–12 months | RDA | 1.2 g/kg | 11 g |
| 1–3 years | RDA | 1.05 g/kg | 13 g |
| 4–8 years | RDA | 0.95 g/kg | 19 g |

Breast milk or formula supplies all protein needs for the first 6 months. After 6 months, meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, beans, lentils and tofu supply protein along with iron and zinc. Protein deficiency is rare in children with adequate energy intake and access to these foods; very high protein intakes offer no advantage. The Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range for protein is 5–20% of calories for children 1–3 years and 10–30% for 4–18 years.

## Fat and carbohydrate

Fat should **not** be restricted in infancy: the AI for total fat is 31 g/day at 0–6 months and 30 g/day at 7–12 months (about 40–55% of energy), reflecting breast milk. For children 1–3 years, fat should provide 30–40% of energy, and 25–35% from 4 years. Carbohydrate RDA is 130 g/day from 1 year; the AI is 60 g/day at 0–6 months and 95 g/day at 7–12 months. Total fibre AI is 19 g/day for 1–3 years and 25 g/day for 4–8 years (none set for infants).

## Water

Total water AI (from all beverages and foods) is 0.7 L/day at 0–6 months (from breast milk/formula), 0.8 L/day at 7–12 months, 1.3 L/day at 1–3 years and 1.7 L/day at 4–8 years.
