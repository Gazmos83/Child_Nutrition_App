# Dietary Reference Intakes: Vitamins and Minerals for Infants and Young Children

Applies to: children from birth to 60 months. Source: National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (Institute of Medicine) Dietary Reference Intake reports (1997–2011, including the 2011 calcium and vitamin D report), as summarised in the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements nutrient recommendation tables.

## How to read the values

Values are Recommended Dietary Allowances (RDA) unless marked AI (Adequate Intake, used for infants and where evidence is limited). Tolerable Upper Intake Levels (UL) are the maximum daily intakes unlikely to cause harm; supplements should not push intake above them without medical supervision. The 0–6 month values reflect the composition of breast milk.

## Iron

| Age | Value |
|---|---|
| 0–6 months | 0.27 mg (AI) |
| 7–12 months | **11 mg** |
| 1–3 years | **7 mg** |
| 4–8 years | 10 mg |

UL: 40 mg/day for 0–13 years. Iron needs jump sharply at 7–12 months as birth stores are exhausted; iron-fortified cereal, meat, poultry, fish and legumes are the food sources.

## Zinc

| Age | Value |
|---|---|
| 0–6 months | 2 mg (AI) |
| 7–12 months | 3 mg |
| 1–3 years | 3 mg |
| 4–8 years | 5 mg |

UL: 4 mg (0–6 mo), 5 mg (7–12 mo), 7 mg (1–3 y), 12 mg (4–8 y). Meat, poultry, fortified cereals, beans and dairy are the main sources.

## Calcium

| Age | Value |
|---|---|
| 0–6 months | 200 mg (AI) |
| 7–12 months | 260 mg (AI) |
| 1–3 years | **700 mg** |
| 4–8 years | **1,000 mg** |

UL: 1,000 mg (0–6 mo), 1,500 mg (7–12 mo), 2,500 mg (1–8 y). A cup (240 ml) of milk or fortified soy milk provides about 300 mg; yoghurt, cheese, tofu set with calcium and fortified foods contribute.

## Vitamin D

| Age | Value |
|---|---|
| 0–12 months | **400 IU (10 µg)** (AI) |
| 1–8 years | **600 IU (15 µg)** |

UL: 1,000 IU (0–6 mo), 1,500 IU (7–12 mo), 2,500 IU (1–3 y), 3,000 IU (4–8 y). Breastfed infants need a supplement; fortified milk supplies about 100 IU per cup after 12 months.

## Vitamin A

| Age | Value |
|---|---|
| 0–6 months | 400 µg RAE (AI) |
| 7–12 months | 500 µg RAE (AI) |
| 1–3 years | 300 µg RAE |
| 4–8 years | 400 µg RAE |

UL: 600 µg (0–3 y), 900 µg (4–8 y) of preformed vitamin A. Orange and dark-green vegetables, liver (sparingly), dairy and eggs are sources.

## Vitamin C

0–6 months 40 mg (AI); 7–12 months 50 mg (AI); 1–3 years 15 mg; 4–8 years 25 mg. UL: 400 mg (1–3 y), 650 mg (4–8 y). Vitamin C in the same meal improves absorption of plant iron.

## B vitamins

- **Vitamin B12:** 0.4 µg (0–6 mo, AI); 0.5 µg (7–12 mo, AI); 0.9 µg (1–3 y); 1.2 µg (4–8 y). Found only in animal foods and fortified products — a supplement is essential for vegan infants and children and for breastfed infants of vegan mothers.
- **Folate:** 65 µg DFE (0–6 mo); 80 µg (7–12 mo); 150 µg (1–3 y); 200 µg (4–8 y).
- **Thiamin/riboflavin/niacin/B6:** small amounts easily met by varied diets and fortified cereals.

## Iodine

110 µg (0–6 mo, AI); 130 µg (7–12 mo, AI); 90 µg (1–8 y). Sources: iodised salt used sparingly in family cooking, dairy, eggs, fish. UL: 200 µg (1–3 y), 300 µg (4–8 y).

## Fluoride

0.01 mg (0–6 mo, AI); 0.5 mg (7–12 mo, AI); 0.7 mg (1–3 y, AI); 1 mg (4–8 y, AI). Fluoride comes mainly from fluoridated water; supplementation decisions depend on local water fluoride and should be made with a dentist or clinician.

## Sodium and potassium

Sodium AI: 110 mg (0–6 mo), 370 mg (7–12 mo), 800 mg (1–3 y), 1,000 mg (4–8 y); Chronic Disease Risk Reduction intake: reduce if above 1,200 mg (1–3 y) or 1,500 mg (4–8 y). Potassium AI: 400 mg (0–6 mo), 860 mg (7–12 mo), 2,000 mg (1–3 y), 2,300 mg (4–8 y).
