# Healthy Beverage Consumption in Early Childhood: Consensus Recommendations (2019)

Applies to: children from birth to 5 years (0–60 months). Source: Healthy Eating Research consensus statement, *Healthy Beverage Consumption in Early Childhood: Recommendations from Key National Health and Nutrition Organizations*, 2019, developed with and endorsed by the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP), American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry, Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics and American Heart Association.

## The short version

For children under 5, the only drinks they need are **breast milk or infant formula in the first year**, and **water and plain milk after that**. Everything else — juice, flavoured milk, toddler formulas, plant-based drinks, sugary drinks, caffeinated drinks — is either unnecessary or actively discouraged.

## Birth to 6 months

Breast milk or iron-fortified infant formula only. **No water, juice, cow's milk or other drinks.** Water is not needed and can be harmful in young infants.

## 6 to 12 months

Continue breast milk or infant formula. Small amounts of **plain water can be introduced at about 6 months — roughly 4–8 ounces (120–240 ml) a day**, offered in a cup with meals as complementary foods begin. **No juice, no cow's milk as a drink, no plant-based milks, no flavoured or sweetened drinks.** Small amounts of cow's milk in cooked foods (for example in cereal or yoghurt) are acceptable from 6 months for infants without a milk allergy.

## 12 to 24 months

- **Whole cow's milk** becomes an appropriate drink at 12 months. Recommended amount: **about 2 to 3 cups (16–24 ounces, 480–720 ml) per day**. More than this displaces iron-rich foods and raises the risk of iron deficiency.
- **Water:** about **1 to 4 cups (240–960 ml) per day**, depending on activity and climate.
- Whole (full-fat) milk is generally recommended in the second year; a clinician may advise reduced-fat milk for a toddler who is gaining weight excessively or has a family history of obesity or heart disease.
- Juice is best avoided; if given, no more than 4 ounces per day of 100% juice (see the AAP juice policy).
- Toddler "formulas" and "transition" milks are **not needed** for healthy toddlers.

## 2 to 5 years

- **Milk:** about **2 to 2½ cups (16–20 ounces, 480–600 ml) per day**, switching to **skim or low-fat (1%) milk at age 2**.
- **Water:** about **1 to 5 cups per day** (1–4 cups at 2–3 years; 1½–5 cups at 4–5 years).
- Juice: no more than 4 ounces per day at 2–3 years, 4–6 ounces at 4–5 years; whole fruit preferred.

## Plant-based milks

Unsweetened, fortified **soy milk** is the only plant-based milk considered a suitable nutritional alternative to cow's milk for young children who cannot drink dairy. Almond, oat, rice, coconut and similar drinks are low in protein and other nutrients and should be used only under a clinician's or dietitian's guidance. No plant-based milk should replace breast milk or infant formula in the first year.

## Drinks to avoid entirely under 5

- **Sugar-sweetened beverages** (soda, sports drinks, fruit drinks, sweetened waters, sweet tea).
- **Flavoured milks** (chocolate, strawberry) — added sugar.
- **Caffeinated drinks** of any kind, including tea, coffee and energy drinks.
- **Drinks with low-calorie sweeteners** — not recommended for young children.
- **Toddler milks / "growing-up" formulas** — unnecessary and often sweetened.

## Why milk amounts are limited

Milk is a good source of calcium, vitamin D, protein and fat, but it is low in iron. Toddlers who drink well over 24 ounces a day fill up on milk, eat fewer iron-rich foods and are at risk of iron-deficiency anaemia and constipation.
