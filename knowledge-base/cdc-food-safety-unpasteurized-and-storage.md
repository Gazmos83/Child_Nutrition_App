# CDC Food Safety for Infants and Young Children: Unpasteurised Products, Formula Preparation and Breast Milk Storage

Applies to: infants and children from birth to 60 months. Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — *Food Safety: People at Risk (children under 5)*, *Raw Milk*, *Infant Formula Preparation and Storage*, and *Proper Storage and Preparation of Breast Milk*, 2024; formula preparation temperatures consistent with WHO/FAO *Safe preparation, storage and handling of powdered infant formula* (2007).

## Why young children need extra care

Children under 5 have immature immune systems and small bodies, so foodborne infections (*Salmonella*, *E. coli* O157, *Listeria*, *Campylobacter*, *Cronobacter*) hit them harder — dehydration comes quickly, and *E. coli* O157 can cause kidney failure (haemolytic uraemic syndrome) in young children. Prevention rests on a few firm rules.

## Never give unpasteurised products

- **Raw (unpasteurised) milk** from any animal, and **soft cheeses made from raw milk** (some brie, camembert, feta, queso fresco, blue-veined cheeses). Choose products labelled "pasteurised".
- **Unpasteurised juice or cider**, including fresh-pressed juice sold refrigerated unless labelled pasteurised.
- **Raw honey** is separately prohibited under 12 months because of botulism spores — see the honey guidance.

## Cook thoroughly

- **Eggs:** cook until both yolk and white are firm; no runny eggs, no raw egg in homemade mayonnaise, batter or desserts. Pasteurised egg products are an alternative.
- **Meat and poultry:** ground meat to 160°F (71°C), poultry to 165°F (74°C), whole cuts to 145°F (63°C) with a rest. No rare meat for young children.
- **Fish and shellfish:** to 145°F (63°C); no raw or undercooked seafood.
- **Raw sprouts** (alfalfa, bean, clover) should not be served to young children.
- **Hot dogs and deli meats** should be reheated until steaming hot because of *Listeria*, and remain a choking hazard for under-4s.

## Infant formula preparation

- Wash hands, and clean and sanitise bottles, nipples and preparation surfaces.
- Use water from a safe source. For infants **under 3 months, premature, or with weakened immunity**, CDC and WHO advise preparing powdered formula with **water heated to at least 158°F (70°C)** — boiled and cooled no more than 30 minutes — then cooling the bottle to feeding temperature, because powdered formula is not sterile and can contain *Cronobacter*.
- Use the scoop provided and the exact water-to-powder ratio on the label. **Never dilute formula** to make it last longer or concentrate it to add calories; both are dangerous.
- Use prepared formula **within 2 hours** of preparation, or within 1 hour once feeding has started; refrigerate unused prepared formula and use within 24 hours. Discard leftovers from a bottle the baby has fed from.
- Do not warm bottles in a microwave (uneven hot spots); use warm water.

## Breast milk storage

- Freshly expressed milk: **room temperature up to 4 hours; refrigerator (40°F/4°C) up to 4 days; freezer (0°F/−18°C) best within 6 months, acceptable up to 12 months.**
- Thawed milk: use within 24 hours in the refrigerator; within 2 hours at room temperature; **never refreeze**.
- Leftover milk from a feed: use within 2 hours or discard.
- Thaw in the refrigerator or under warm running water; do not microwave. Label containers with the date and use the oldest first.

## Everyday kitchen hygiene

Wash hands before handling food and after touching raw meat, eggs or nappies. Keep raw meat separate from ready-to-eat foods. Refrigerate perishable food within 2 hours (1 hour if above 90°F/32°C). Wash fruits and vegetables under running water. Discard food a young child has partly eaten rather than saving it.

## Signs that need medical attention

Vomiting or diarrhoea with signs of dehydration (few wet nappies, dry mouth, no tears, sunken eyes, unusual sleepiness), blood in stool, fever in an infant under 3 months, or a child who cannot keep fluids down should be seen promptly.
