# Vegetarian and Vegan Diets for Infants and Young Children

Applies to: children from birth to 60 months. Source: American Academy of Pediatrics, *Pediatric Nutrition*, 8th edition (2020), chapter on nutritional aspects of vegetarian diets; and the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics Position Paper *Vegetarian Diets* (2016), which the AAP guidance cites.

## Can young children thrive on a vegetarian or vegan diet?

Yes, **when the diet is well planned**. Appropriately planned vegetarian diets — including vegan diets — can meet the needs of infants, toddlers and preschoolers and support normal growth. The key word is *planned*: the younger the child and the more restrictive the diet, the less margin there is for error, and a few nutrients need deliberate attention. Families following a vegan diet for an infant should involve their paediatrician and, ideally, a registered dietitian.

## The first year

- **Breast milk** is the ideal food for a vegetarian or vegan infant; breastfeeding mothers on a vegan diet need a reliable **vitamin B12** source (supplement or fortified foods) so that their milk contains enough.
- If not breastfed, use a **commercial infant formula**. For vegan families, **soy-based infant formula** is the appropriate choice. **Home-made formulas, and plant milks such as almond, oat, rice or coconut milk, must never be used as the main milk for an infant under 12 months** — they lack the protein, fat, energy and micronutrients infants need, and cases of severe malnutrition have resulted.
- Complementary foods from about 6 months follow the usual pattern with a vegetarian emphasis: iron-fortified infant cereal, pureed beans and lentils, tofu, smooth nut and seed butters (thinned, never whole nuts), well-cooked egg and yoghurt if lacto-ovo vegetarian, and a variety of vegetables and fruits. Vitamin C-rich foods at the same meal improve iron absorption.

## Nutrients that need attention

- **Vitamin B12** — found naturally only in animal foods. **All vegan children need a B12 supplement or reliably fortified foods**; deficiency in infancy causes anaemia and lasting neurological damage. Lacto-ovo vegetarians usually obtain enough from dairy and eggs.
- **Iron** — plant (non-haem) iron is absorbed less well. Offer iron-fortified cereals, beans, lentils, tofu, and pair with vitamin C; avoid tea at meals. Breastfed vegetarian infants follow the same 4-month iron-supplement advice as other breastfed infants; screening at 12 months is especially important.
- **Zinc** — beans, lentils, tofu, nut and seed butters, fortified cereals; soaking and sprouting legumes improves absorption.
- **Calcium and vitamin D** — dairy (if included), calcium-set tofu, fortified soy milk (after 12 months), leafy greens; a vitamin D supplement of 400 IU (infants) or 600 IU (1 year+) is usually needed.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids (DHA)** — sources such as flaxseed, chia and walnuts (ground) provide ALA; a DHA supplement from algal oil may be considered for vegan children, particularly if the mother's diet lacked DHA.
- **Protein** — usually adequate if energy is adequate and a variety of legumes, soy, grains, nuts and seeds is eaten daily; young vegan children may need slightly more protein than the RDA because of lower digestibility.
- **Energy** — plant-based diets are bulky and high in fibre, and toddlers have small stomachs. Include energy-dense foods (avocado, nut and seed butters, oils, tofu, full-fat soy products) and do not overload with high-fibre foods that fill a child before needs are met.
- **Iodine** — iodised salt in modest amounts in family cooking, or a supplement, since vegan diets without dairy or fish can be low.

## Milk after 12 months

For toddlers who do not drink cow's milk, **fortified, unsweetened soy milk** is the recommended alternative. Other plant milks are low in protein and should not be the main milk without dietitian advice. Aim for the same 2–3 cups a day as dairy milk in the second year.

## Monitoring

Growth (weight, length/height and head circumference plotted on WHO/CDC charts) is the best indicator that a vegetarian or vegan diet is meeting a child's needs. Check-ups should review B12, iron and vitamin D status when indicated.
