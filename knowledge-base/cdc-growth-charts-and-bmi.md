# CDC: Using Growth Charts and BMI for Children Birth to 5 Years

Applies to: children from birth to 60 months. Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *Growth Charts — WHO Growth Standards Are Recommended for Use in the U.S. for Infants and Children 0 to 2 Years of Age* and *About Child & Teen BMI*, 2022; consistent with American Academy of Pediatrics recommendations.

## Which chart for which age

- **Birth to 2 years:** use the **WHO Child Growth Standards** (weight-for-age, length-for-age, weight-for-length, head circumference-for-age). The CDC and AAP recommend the WHO standards for this age because they describe growth of healthy breastfed infants under optimal conditions and better reflect how infants should grow.
- **2 years and older:** use the **CDC growth charts** (weight-for-age, stature-for-age, **BMI-for-age**). Height is measured standing from age 2.

Because age drives expectations so strongly, age should be recorded precisely — in months for children under 2, and in years and months thereafter — rather than rounded to whole years.

## Measuring accurately

Weigh infants naked or in a dry nappy on an infant scale; measure length lying flat on a length board with two people; measure head circumference with a non-stretch tape. From age 2, weigh in light clothing without shoes and measure standing height with a wall-mounted stadiometer. Poor measurement is the most common cause of a "growth problem" that turns out not to exist.

## Percentiles

A child's measurement is plotted against age to find the percentile — the percentage of children of the same age and sex who are smaller. The 50th percentile is the median. Being at the 10th or the 90th percentile is normal for many children; what matters is **consistency over time**. On the WHO charts, the outer lines correspond to z-scores of −2 and +2 (roughly the 2nd and 98th percentiles).

## What raises concern

- Weight-for-length (under 2) or BMI-for-age (2 and over) **above the 95th percentile** (obesity) or **between the 85th and 95th** (overweight), on CDC charts.
- Weight-for-age or weight-for-length **below the 5th percentile** (underweight), or length/height-for-age below the 5th percentile (short stature), on CDC charts; below −2 z-scores on WHO charts.
- **Crossing two or more major percentile lines** up or down, or a flat weight line over several months in infancy.
- A head circumference that is crossing percentiles or is far out of line with length.

Any of these prompts a clinical review of feeding, health and family growth pattern; they are not diagnoses on their own.

## BMI in children

BMI is weight (kg) divided by height (m) squared. For children, BMI is meaningful **only as a percentile for age and sex**, because healthy BMI changes with age: it rises in infancy, falls to a low around age 5–6, then rises again. **Adult BMI cut-offs (25, 30) do not apply to children.** Routine BMI-for-age screening begins at age 2. Below age 2, weight-for-length is used instead.

CDC BMI-for-age categories (age 2 and over):

- **Underweight:** below the 5th percentile
- **Healthy weight:** 5th to below the 85th percentile
- **Overweight:** 85th to below the 95th percentile
- **Obesity:** 95th percentile or above

## Normal growth patterns

Infants typically double birth weight by about 4–5 months and triple it by 12 months, and grow about 25 cm in length in the first year. Growth slows sharply in the second year and preschool years (roughly 2–3 kg and 6–9 cm a year), and appetite often appears to fall accordingly — this is expected. Breastfed infants may gain more slowly after 3–4 months than formula-fed infants; this is normal on the WHO charts.

## When to measure

Well-child visits at birth, 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24 and 30 months and yearly from 3 years include measurement and plotting. Growth over time, interpreted by a clinician who knows the child, is far more informative than any single number.
