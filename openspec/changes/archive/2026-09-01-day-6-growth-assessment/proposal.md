## Why

Day 5's manual check 7 ("what's her BMI?") showed the assistant handing a parent a bare
BMI number with a note that the percentile "is not computed". For a child that number
means nothing on its own: weight, length, and BMI are only interpretable as **age- and
sex-specific percentiles** on a growth chart — the knowledge base already says so
(`cdc-growth-charts-and-bmi.md`, `who-child-growth-standards-2006.md`), and the `Sex`
enum exists precisely for "growth-standard lookups". The WHO Child Growth Standards
(2006) cover 0–60 months per sex — exactly this app's domain — and publish the LMS
parameters that turn a measurement into a z-score with table arithmetic. This change adds
that arithmetic as one more deterministic tool.

**Scope: [Stretch]. Day 6 of 7, morning, before the evaluation, security, and logging
work.** The MVP asked for BMI, which exists; this closes a correctness gap rather than
adding a feature line. Time-boxed to half a day; it gates nothing on Day 6 or Day 7.
Applied after `day-5-feeding-log-and-gaps` is archived.

## What Changes

- **A new deterministic tool, `assess_growth`** — from age in months, sex, weight, and
  (optionally) length/height it returns, for each WHO indicator it can compute, the
  z-score, percentile, a classification band using WHO's published cut-offs, WHO's own
  label where WHO defines one, and the reference values (−2 SD, median, +2 SD) for that
  age and sex. Indicators: weight-for-age, length/height-for-age, weight-for-length/height,
  BMI-for-age. No number is generated by the model (architecture principle; hard rule 7).
- **Missing data degrades, never errors** — without a height only weight-for-age is
  computed; the other indicators are reported as skipped with a parent-facing reason. A
  length outside the WHO weight-for-length table's range is likewise skipped, not failed.
- **Reference data with provenance** — a committed table of WHO 2006 LMS parameters per
  indicator, sex, and completed month (or 0.1 cm of length), generated by a re-runnable
  script from WHO's published `.xlsx` tables, with source URL, retrieval date, and file
  checksums recorded (hard rule 5 provenance carries into tool output).
- **`calculate_bmi` is unchanged in contract**; its note and tool description now point
  parents and the router to `assess_growth` for the percentile.
- **Routing and answer prompts** name growth percentiles so "is her weight normal for her
  age?" and "what's his BMI?" reach the tool rather than the guideline search; the answer
  prompt states percentiles verbatim and explains a WHO label only when the band is not
  the expected range, in the direct voice reserved for safety topics.
- **UI** — a growth card in chat: one row per indicator with the measurement, percentile,
  a marker on the −3…+3 SD track, WHO reference values, and WHO's label when present;
  skipped rows show their reason. Copy is direct (Day 5 Decision 13): no weight-status
  wordplay, no diagnosis, a note that the WHO standard is a reference, not a diagnosis,
  with a pointer to plotting the trend with the pediatrician.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `growth-assessment`: placing a child's weight, length/height, and BMI on the WHO Child
  Growth Standards by age in months and sex — z-scores, percentiles, WHO cut-off bands,
  reference values, graceful handling of missing or out-of-range measurements, and the
  provenance of the reference table.

### Modified Capabilities

- `deterministic-nutrient-tools`: `assess_growth` joins the tool set under the existing
  no-model-arithmetic and validate-before-compute requirements; `calculate_bmi`'s result
  now directs the reader to `assess_growth` for interpretation.
- `intent-routing`: a question about a child's growth percentile, BMI, or whether a
  measurement is normal for their age routes to a tool.
- `chat-ui`: a growth-assessment result renders as a structured card with direct copy.

## Non-goals

- **CDC 2000 growth references** — WHO 2006 covers the whole 0–60-month range; the CDC
  BMI-for-age categories quoted in the KB (2 years and over) are cited by RAG, not
  computed.
- **Age in days or a date of birth** — the form supplies completed months; the tool uses
  WHO's monthly tables and says so. Exact-age (daily) tables are a [Stretch] refinement.
- **Head circumference, arm circumference, skinfolds, velocity standards** — not asked
  for, no form fields.
- **Trend analysis across measurements** — needs stored history (Day 6 persistence);
  the card tells the parent that trends matter and who can plot them.
- **Feeding the assessment into meal-plan targets** — a low or high percentile does not
  change calorie or protein targets in this change; that is a clinical judgement the
  disclaimer defers to the pediatrician.
- **WHO's posture correction (0.7 cm between recumbent length and standing height)** —
  the form's single field is treated as length under 24 months and height from 24 months;
  the result notes this.
